\begin{abstract}
This short technical note recalls the classical theory of quadratic residues (Legendre and Jacobi symbols, Euler’s criterion, and the law of quadratic reciprocity) and explicitly distinguishes it from two bodies of work that employ the word ``residue'' in different senses: the Stage~1/2 algebraic residue certificates for an explicit sextic fourfold in the HODGE repository, and the local residue-class analysis (modulo~42) underlying the truncated-operator sieve barrier for twin primes. The note also records the limited appearance of quadratic character sums in certain weighted or spectral sieve approaches and confirms that the classical Selberg parity barrier is orthogonal to quadratic residuosity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Classical quadratic residues}

Let \(p\) be an odd prime and let \(a\) be an integer not divisible by \(p\). Then \(a\) is a \emph{quadratic residue} modulo \(p\) if the congruence
\[
x^2 \equiv a \pmod{p}
\]
has a solution; otherwise \(a\) is a quadratic non-residue. Equivalently, the Legendre symbol satisfies
\[
\Bigl(\frac{a}{p}\Bigr) = 1
\]
when \(a\) is a residue and \(-1\) when \(a\) is a non-residue. (The symbol is defined to be \(0\) when \(p\mid a\).)

Exactly \((p-1)/2\) residue classes and \((p-1)/2\) non-residue classes exist modulo \(p\). The symbol is completely multiplicative:
\[
\Bigl(\frac{ab}{p}\Bigr) = \Bigl(\frac{a}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{b}{p}\Bigr).
\]
Euler’s criterion states that
\[
\Bigl(\frac{a}{p}\Bigr) \equiv a^{(p-1)/2} \pmod{p}.
\]
For distinct odd primes \(p\) and \(q\) the law of quadratic reciprocity asserts
\[
\Bigl(\frac{p}{q}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{q}{p}\Bigr) = (-1)^{(p-1)/2\cdot(q-1)/2}.
\]
Supplementary laws determine \(\bigl(\frac{-1}{p}\bigr)\) and \(\bigl(\frac{2}{p}\bigr)\).

The Jacobi symbol \(\bigl(\frac{a}{n}\bigr)\) extends the Legendre symbol to odd positive integers \(n\). It remains completely multiplicative and may be evaluated in polynomial time without factoring \(n\), yet the equality \(\bigl(\frac{a}{n}\bigr)=1\) does not imply that \(a\) is a square modulo \(n\) when \(n\) is composite.

\section{Separation from the local sieve barrier}

The sieve-barrier papers (including the published bbff record \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24191007}{10.5281/zenodo.24191007}) analyse local residue classes modulo~42. Those papers claim that the truncated operator \(\Omega_R\) is blind to prime factors larger than the sieve level, and that the associated generating function therefore collapses identically on genuine twin primes and on high-magnitude pseudo-primes that lie in the same residue class. That generating-function collapse is a named hypothesis; it is not proved in this note, and it is not proved in the companion Lean repository, because those sieve-theoretic objects are not formalized there. The quadratic commutation barrier preserves the classical Selberg parity error. The obstruction claimed in those papers is therefore purely modular and is independent of the Legendre or Jacobi symbol.

Related modular constraints appear in the \(p^2q\)-jump work (concept DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22713135}{10.5281/zenodo.22713135}, repository \href{https://github.com/BenFrohman/p2q-jumps-lean}{BenFrohman/p2q-jumps-lean}) and in the Collatz teaming lemmas; again the primary objects are not quadratic residuosity symbols.

\section{Quadratic residuosity in the sieve literature}

Character sums involving the Jacobi symbol appear in certain weighted or spectral sieve approaches to the twin-prime problem, where they detect quadratic non-residues that obstruct particular decompositions. The classical parity barrier itself (Selberg and later authors) is orthogonal to quadratic residuosity. The local sieve-barrier papers correctly isolate the blindness of the truncated operator to large prime factors as the operative obstruction, independently of Legendre symbols.

\section{Companion Lean interface}

The public repository \href{https://github.com/BenFrohman/quadratic-residues-lean}{BenFrohman/quadratic-residues-lean} supplies a polished Lean~4 interface to Mathlib’s formalizations of the Legendre symbol, quadratic reciprocity, the Jacobi symbol, and Dirichlet characters, together with the following short kernel-checked modular observation. The official kernel certificate is GitHub Actions run~\href{https://github.com/BenFrohman/quadratic-residues-lean/actions/runs/45320294722}{45320294722} on merge commit \href{https://github.com/BenFrohman/quadratic-residues-lean/commit/225d4e47c7d232b6a23bdfe326dd93bd2ec41e76}{\texttt{225d4e47c7d232b6a23bdfe326dd93bd2ec41e76}} (23~November~2026).

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:mod42}
The Lean theorem
\begin{center}
{\footnotesize\ttfamily Frohman.QuadraticResidues.\\
mod42\_determines\_divisibility\_by\_two\_three\_seven}
\end{center}
states: for \(n,m:\mathbb{N}\), if \(n \mathbin{\%} 42 = m \mathbin{\%} 42\), then
\[
(2 \mid n \leftrightarrow 2 \mid m)\ \land\ (3 \mid n \leftrightarrow 3 \mid m)\ \land\ (7 \mid n \leftrightarrow 7 \mid m).
\]
In the Lean statement:
\begin{center}
\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{}}
theorem mod42\_determines\_divisibility\_by\_two\_three\_seven\\
\quad (n m : \(\mathbb{N}\)) (h : n \% 42 = m \% 42) :\\
\quad (2 \(\mid\) n \(\leftrightarrow\) 2 \(\mid\) m) \(\land\) (3 \(\mid\) n \(\leftrightarrow\) 3 \(\mid\) m) \(\land\) (7 \(\mid\) n \(\leftrightarrow\) 7 \(\mid\) m).
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The official certificate records that this theorem depends only on the axiom \texttt{propext}.
\end{theorem}

The claim, coming from the sieve-barrier papers, that the truncated operator \(\Omega_R\) is blind above the sieve level independently of the Legendre or Jacobi symbol, and that the associated generating function therefore collapses identically on genuine twin primes and on high-magnitude pseudo-primes in the same residue class modulo~42, is recorded in that repository as a named hypothesis; it is not proved there, and it is not proved in this note, because those sieve-theoretic objects are not formalized in the present development. The present note is the accompanying explanatory deposit.
